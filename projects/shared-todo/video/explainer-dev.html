TSP Direct messages: HPKE-Auth to the recipient's X25519 key, signed with Ed25519, CESR framing, carried in the trusttasks.org/binding/tsp/0.1 envelope. They travel over a WebSocket relay.

A did:key (Ed25519) for every device and every list, made locally. X25519 encryption keys are derived from it, as the P2P Trust Stack does.

A list is a small VTC

The list has its own DID

It is made on the creator's device, and that key signs everything about membership. One key holds it today; M-of-N governance would replace that.

A membership VC

Issued by the list's DID to the member's DID, with a role: admin, editor or viewer.

An acknowledgement VC back

Issued by the member to the list's DID. The two one-way credentials make the bidirectional edge, much like vtc/members/vmc.

A roster, signed by the list's key

{ type: "todo/roster", issuer: <list DID>, version: 7, members: [{ did, name, role, membershipId, joinedAt, removedAt?, keptOps? }], proof }

A device accepts a roster only if the list signed it, its version is higher than the one held, and it keeps every earlier removal. So a replayed old roster can't undo a removal. A removal lists the ops it keeps, so every device converges however things arrive. Names and roles come only from here.

All of them are one-way, because either side may be offline. They live in our own namespace, https://toip.tools/spec/todo/…/0.1. They are shaped after the registry specs but don't conform to them. Moving them onto the registry is the obvious next step.

The path of one change

1 · Sign the op

The op is signed with eddsa-jcs-2022, by itself, so anyone can forward it and it still proves its author.

2 · Wrap it

A todo/op Trust Task document, with its own proof, issuer, recipient and issuedAt.

3 · Seal it

A TSP Direct message, sealed separately for each member: HPKE-Auth, then signed with Ed25519.

4 · The relay

DID challenge auth: sign nonce|relayURL. It stores the message until the recipient acknowledges it. It sees only who, when and how big.

5 · Receive

TspConsumer opens it, then checks freshness, the replay guard and the proof (SPEC §7.2). Then come the app's own checks.

Offline is normal. If the relay can't be reached, the sealed messages wait in the device's outbox in IndexedDB. If the recipient is offline, the relay holds them, in SQLite, until they're acknowledged. Delivery is at least once, and devices drop duplicates by (author, op id).

Every device decides for itself

  1. Well formed, including a private item's sealed part
  2. For this list
  3. Dated no more than 5 minutes in the future
  4. The op's Data Integrity proof verifies, and its issuer is its author
  5. A direct op came from its author, as TSP authenticated; a forwarded op is labeled with who forwarded it
  6. The membership VC verifies, was issued by the list, is for the author, and is the one the op names
  7. The roster lists that credential for the author, and it hasn't been removed; after a removal, only the ops the removal listed in keptOps stay valid
  8. The item exists; if it isn't known yet, the op is held, not refused
  9. The list's Rego rules allow it

The Rego is compiled with opa build -t wasm and evaluated with @open-policy-agent/opa-wasm on each device. The trust-tasks registry uses Rego too.

No server, but the same list everywhere

Convergence

Each device rebuilds every item from its accepted ops, in (at, author DID, op id) order. The first add creates the item, the last check or edit wins, and a delete is final.

A test applies 6 ops in all 720 arrival orders and asserts one result.

Private items

The text is encrypted under a fresh item key (ChaCha20-Poly1305). The key is wrapped to each reader's X25519 key with HPKE base mode, the same RFC 9180 suite TSP uses.

Non-readers still verify the op and its permission, without reading it. They see that it exists, who wrote it, and who can read it.

Tested against a real relay

The integration tests run several devices over real WebSockets. They cover joining while the inviter is offline, reused and expired invitations, someone else's credential, a replayed old roster, revocation with history kept, offline outboxes, restarts, and a check that the relay's database never contains item text.

Where we guessed, and where we'd like your input

Where we deviated
  • did:key, not did:peer:4 or did:scid:vh
  • No TSP RFI/RFA handshake: relationships start at the first Direct message, because the TS binding carries only Direct messages
  • Our own task types, not registry specs
  • A VTC held by a single key, with no governance document
  • Our own WebSocket mailbox, not Routed or Nested Mode
  • Interop with the P2P Trust Stack's own implementation hasn't been tested
Questions for you
  • Which registry specs should these messages become, and what's missing from the registry?
  • Is a signed, versioned roster the right shape for VTC membership state, or should it be a status list plus credentials?
  • What are the plans for Routed and Nested Mode in the TypeScript libraries?
  • Would you test interop against your TSP and Trust Task implementations?
  • What would multi-device per person look like: delegation from a person's DID to device keys?
